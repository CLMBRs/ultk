Synthesis. The upward/downward learning asymmetry is mostly not a property of the learner's treatment of individual truth sets. It is carried by which meanings the grammar samples — specifically their class separability in input space — plus measurement noise in the directional degrees. The dominant mediator (class separation) is complement-symmetric, which is exactly why T1 finds no within-pair difference while T2 finds strong population-level mediation. The two results are one coherent story.

Ground truth

verified facts every analysis builds on (HANDOFF §2)
  • Data: 8,005 training runs (7,172 trained; 2,000 unique expressions), CSV verified byte-for-byte against the MLflow Postgres backend.
  • Degree: degree = max of the four clipped directional senses; exact for 100% of rows.
  • Headline correlation: monotonicity–AUC r = −0.333 (published −0.3453 predates a post-publication fix to the monotonicity computation; published Table 6 reproduces to ≤ 0.2%).
  • Length: leaf r = +0.234 / function r = +0.219 with AUC; monotonicity's unique R² (0.089) ≈ 3.4× length's (0.026).
  • Composition control: the degree effect survives length + all 11 operator counts (β −540/SD, mixed p = 2e-38). Not an operator artifact.
  • Directional asymmetry (Q2's subject): downward β −571/SD (p = 1e-39) vs upward +80/SD (n.s.) under full controls.
  • The gap (Q3's subject): only 1.7% of expressions have degree in (0, 0.15] vs 8.8% at exactly 0; mechanism traced to bimodality-on-crisp-inputs + equality-type predicates (review §9i).

The three questions

Q1 · How does the grammar shape monotonicity?

Open — plan ready

The grammar affects monotonicity only by steering which meanings get sampled. Design (EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md, now actually ported): one maximal operator inventory (add domain leaf M + integer constants), randomized Dirichlet production weights per batch, ~60k expressions, ZOIB regressions + grammar response surface with Sobol indices. Seven pre-registered predictions in plan §5.4. Next: Phase 0 (extend grammar.yml, re-derive anchor quantifiers).

Q2 · What drives the upward/downward learning asymmetry?

Major progress — T1 & T2 done

The puzzle: sigmoid + BCE training is symmetric under label complement, and the complement of an up-set is a down-set — so pure decision-boundary geometry cannot produce a direction effect. Any real asymmetry must come from sample composition, the encoding, the example-sampling, or the measure. This session tested the boundary-level channel directly (T1) and the input-statistics channel (T2). See the scoreboard and findings below.

Q3 · The gap in (0, 0.15]: measure, sampling, or both?

Partially answered

Established (review §9i): a joint product — the measure is bimodal on crisp inputs, and the grammar supplies only crisp inputs; equality-type predicates are the only crisp route into the gap. Open items: re-run the family simulation on the real model space; audit the pred ≡ 1 and saturation boundary conditions; grammar predictions (ties to Q1); check persistence under the generalized-switches measure.

Method assumptions

what this session's conclusions rest on

Inherited from the pipeline

  • Training pairs are (model, label) only — the learner never sees the expression; sigmoid + BCE is symmetric under label complement.
  • Training models drawn by generate_batch(M=12, X=16, inclusive=False) with balanced classes (≤ 5,000 per class), per conf/learn.yaml.
  • Degrees are computed on the M4/X4 256-model universe — a different space from the training distribution.
  • Ease of learning = validation-loss AUC, averaged over trained runs per expression × architecture.

Introduced this session

  • Complementarity is verified on 20k sampled training-style models — overwhelming evidence, not logical proof (3 pairs did fail, proving the check is load-bearing).
  • Truth-set statistics estimated on 4,000 fixed models (seed 11); zone-count features are encoding-agnostic summaries, not the literal network input.
  • Regressions are per-expression OLS (runs averaged), matching prior sections — not mixed models; p-values are descriptive.
  • D-member of a pair = larger clipped max(down) − max(up); left/right senses are collapsed by max, as in all prior analyses.
